Pre-production · V1 in development

Surgical visualisation and navigation, designed by physicians.

NavigoSurg is a platform for planning and navigating surgery from a patient's own imaging. Hepatic planning is the first module, not the whole of it.

The module in development, HPS-1, is software that turns a standard triphasic CT into an interactive three-dimensional map of the liver — segmental anatomy, venous architecture, and remnant volume — for surgeons planning living donor transplantation and complex resection.

The clinical problem

Hepatic vascular anatomy is variant, and the planning tools aren't.

Preoperative planning for liver surgery still depends largely on reconstructing three-dimensional vascular anatomy mentally, from axial slices. Variant anatomy is common rather than exceptional. In living donor transplantation — which accounts for the majority of liver transplants performed in India — the tolerance for a misjudged division plane is small. Existing three-dimensional planning systems address this, but most are tied to a specific vendor's hardware or to a workstation inside the radiology department, which is not where the surgical decision gets made.

V1 scope

What V1 is designed to do

V1 is a pilot-grade build with a deliberately narrow scope. What follows is the specification we are building to, not a description of shipped software.

Segmental and venous segmentation

Automated segmentation of liver parenchyma, the Couinaud segments, and the portal and hepatic venous trees from a standard triphasic CT.

Volumetry

Total, lobar and segmental volumes, and future liver remnant volume computed from a planned division plane rather than estimated from a formula.

Virtual resection

Define a resection plane on the three-dimensional model and see the resulting remnant and specimen.

Vessel measurement at the cut plane

Branch diameter and cross-sectional area at the division surface — the measurements that inform anastomosis planning.

Known limits of V1

Arterial tree reconstruction is staged behind a confidence threshold and will not display for every case. Biliary anatomy is limited to the extrahepatic duct. Several outputs are heuristic estimates and are labelled as such in the interface. Every output is intended for review against the source CT, not in place of it.

Approach

Three decisions that shape the product

Measurement at the division plane

Parenchymal and segmental segmentation is increasingly available, including from open-source tooling. Our focus is the layer above it: what the vessels are doing where the surgeon intends to cut.

Cross-platform by design

Built as web software so it opens on a tablet at the bedside or a laptop at a referring centre, rather than on one workstation in one department.

Clinical logic owned in-house

Segmentation is engineering. Deciding which measurements matter, how variant anatomy is classified, and what the interface should refuse to assert is clinical work, and it sits with the founders.

Specified pipeline

How it will work

  1. 01

    DICOM ingest

    Standard triphasic CT, no special acquisition protocol.

  2. 02

    Segmentation

    Parenchyma, Couinaud segments, portal and hepatic venous trees.

  3. 03

    Reconstruction

    Interactive three-dimensional model with cross-reference back to the source slices.

  4. 04

    Planning

    Virtual resection plane, remnant volumetry, vessel measurement at the cut surface.

  5. 05

    Review

    Outputs presented alongside the source CT, with heuristic values flagged as estimates.

Validation

How we intend to demonstrate that it works

Accuracy claims should rest on measurement, so we are not making any yet. Planned standalone performance evaluation covers Dice similarity for parenchymal and segmental segmentation against a radiologist-established reference standard; per-branch detection sensitivity and false-branch rate for the venous trees; and absolute and percentage volumetric error against manual volumetry. A portion of the evaluation dataset is held back from anyone involved in development, and performance will be reported stratified by scanner, slice thickness and contrast timing. Results will be published here when they exist.

Roadmap

The first module of a larger platform

Hepatic planning is where we start, not where we stop. NavigoSurg is designed as a surgical visualisation and navigation platform that extends into further specialties and, in time, into the operating theatre itself.

In development

Funded, under active build

HPS-1 — pre-operative planning for liver resection and living donor transplantation. Segmentation, volumetry, virtual resection and vessel measurement at the division plane.

Planned

Specified, not yet funded

Arterial tree reconstruction with variant classification. Additional surgical specialties, beginning where anatomy is most variable. Patient-specific anatomical models and surgical guides, produced from the same segmentation that drives the planning software — printing through established manufacturing partners rather than in-house.

Long term

Direction, not commitment

Extended-reality surgical rehearsal, and intraoperative visualisation that carries the pre-operative plan to the table. This is a multi-year engineering programme and we describe it as a direction rather than a roadmap item.

Only the first tier is funded and under active development. Everything below it is stated as intent.
